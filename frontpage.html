Middle East · Lead story

Tankers ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said two oil tankers struck naval mines and exploded and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, saying the vessels had been trying to transit an unauthorised route through the narrow waterway. The reported strikes, in the channel that carries a large share of the world’s seaborne oil, deepened fears of a wider energy shock as the Iran war entered a dangerous new phase at sea.

Oil prices held firm on the news, with Brent crude near $95 a barrel, as traders weighed the risk of further disruption to shipping through the strait.

Houthis strike Saudi refinery as Riyadh tower attack injures 11

Yemen’s Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura oil refinery, a day after a Houthi strike on a skyscraper in Riyadh injured 11 people, widening the war’s reach into the kingdom.

Technology & Law

Huawei convicted in US on 10 of 12 counts

A Brooklyn jury found the Chinese telecoms giant guilty of bank fraud, wire fraud, sanctions violations and conspiring to steal trade secrets, acquitting it only of racketeering and obstruction. Sentencing is set for September 2027.

Markets

Dow tops 50,000 as Asia’s chip stocks plunge

The Dow closed above 50,000 for the first time, at 50,018.70, even as South Korea’s KOSPI fell 6.16% and Japan’s Nikkei dropped 2.78% on AI and tariff fears.

Space

First foreign astronaut boards China’s Tiangong station

Shenzhou-24 launched from Jiuquan carrying Pakistan’s Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, who became the first Pakistani in orbit and the first foreign astronaut aboard Tiangong, docking about three and a half hours after liftoff.

Pacific

Australia and Solomon Islands sign pact barring foreign bases

The two countries signed a treaty in Canberra that would preclude a foreign military or security base being established on Solomon Islands, a deal widely seen as aimed at checking China’s influence in the Pacific.

Trade

US proposes new tariffs on 14 trading partners

The Trump administration proposed tariffs of 20% to 40% on 14 major trading partners to fight excess factory capacity, with the highest rates aimed at China. Nothing takes effect before a hearing on 25 January.
